Stay Inside the Window Your PQR Qualified

A welding heat input calculator computes heat input from the arc parameters — current, voltage and travel speed — and, here, clamps every control to the range your PQR qualified, so the value you dial in can never fall outside what the procedure allows. Heat input is the one calculation that's easy to get wrong under pressure, and getting it wrong is how a qualified weld quietly stops being qualified. The helper turns that into something you can see: enter the qualified range for each weld region — root, fill, cap — then move the sliders and watch heat input recompute live in kJ. Because the controls clamp to your window, you can explore combinations freely without ever leaving the qualified range. It's multi-arc aware for tandem and twin setups, and it can pull a WPS's per-region ranges straight from your library. It's a scratch pad — nothing here is saved or written to any sheet.

Set the Range, Then Explore

Your Qualified Window, Per Weld Region

Root, fill and cap each get their own range — the way a PQR actually qualifies them.

Heat input isn't one number for a whole joint — it's qualified region by region. The helper follows that, so what you set matches what your PQR says.

  • Per-region windows: Enter the heat-input range your PQR qualified for each weld region — root, fill and cap — separately, so each is checked against its own limits.
  • Live recompute: Drag the current, voltage and travel-speed sliders and heat input is computed live for the region you're working, in kJ.
  • Explore safely: Because the range is set first, every combination you try is read against the window you qualified — no mental arithmetic to check whether you're still inside it.
You Can't Leave the Window

Sliders Clamped to the Qualified Range

The controls physically stop at your PQR limits — an out-of-range value isn't dialable.

A calculator that lets you compute an out-of-qualification value is a calculator that lets you ship one. This one won't: the window is a hard boundary, not a warning after the fact.

  • Hard limits: The controls clamp to your PQR-qualified range, so you physically can't dial in a combination whose heat input falls outside what the procedure allows.
  • Safe by construction: Rather than flag an error once you've already left the window, the helper makes leaving it impossible — the safe range is the only range you can reach.
  • Confidence under pressure: On the floor or in a review, you can move fast knowing every value the tool will let you produce is inside qualification.
Tandem & Twin-Arc

Multi-Arc Aware, the Way the Standard Expects

A lead and a trail head, each with its own parameters — summed across heads.

Multi-arc processes don't compute like a single arc, and a calculator that pretends otherwise is wrong for exactly the high-productivity setups that need it most.

  • Lead and trail heads: Tandem and twin-arc setups get a lead head and a trail head, each with its own current and voltage.
  • Summed across heads: The total heat input is aggregated across the heads, the way the standard expects a multi-arc weld to be assessed.
  • Same clamped window: The multi-arc total is checked against the same PQR-qualified range, so multi-arc combinations stay inside qualification just like single-arc ones.

Questions & Answers

The Heat Input Calculator, Answered

How it computes, why it clamps, and where the ranges come from.

How does the calculator work out heat input?

It computes heat input live from the arc parameters — current, voltage and travel speed — as you drag the sliders, showing the result in kJ for the weld region you're working. Root, fill and cap each have their own qualified range.

Can it stop me from going out of qualification?

Yes — that's the point. The sliders clamp to your PQR-qualified range, so you physically can't dial in a combination whose heat input falls outside what the procedure allows. Leaving the window isn't possible, rather than flagged after the fact.

Does it handle tandem or twin-arc welding?

Yes. Tandem and twin-arc setups get a lead head and a trail head, each with its own current and voltage, and the total heat input is summed across the heads the way the standard expects — checked against the same qualified window.

Do I have to type in the qualified ranges?

You can, or you can load a parsed WPS from your library and its per-region ranges and processes fill in automatically. The ranges come from the WPS; the arc parameters stay yours to explore.

Does it save what I calculate?

No. It's a scratch pad — a live calculation surface. Nothing you do here is saved or written to any sheet or register, so you can explore freely without changing anything.

Is heat input qualified per region?

Yes. The helper sets and checks root, fill and cap separately, because that's how a PQR qualifies heat input — region by region, each against its own limits.
