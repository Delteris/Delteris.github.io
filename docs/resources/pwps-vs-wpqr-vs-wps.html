pWPS vs WPQR vs WPS:
The Difference Explained.

Three records, one chain of proof: the draft you test, the record that proves it, and the procedure your welders follow.

By the Delteris team · Last reviewed 23 September 2026 · All guides

The Short Answer

A pWPS is the proposed procedure you weld a test piece to. The WPQR records that test and the range of conditions it qualifies. The WPS is the production procedure your welders follow, written inside that range. Draft, proof, production — in that order. A WPS is only ever as valid as the WPQR behind it.

The Chain

How One Becomes the Next

  • 1. Draft the pWPS. Process, parent material, joint, position, filler and intended parameters, laid out as ISO 15609-1 requires for the WPS it will become.
  • 2. Weld and test the coupon. A test piece is welded to the pWPS under an examiner, then examined and tested. Under ISO 15614-1, the resulting WPQR fixes a range of thicknesses, diameters, positions and other variables — not a single point.
  • 3. Write the WPS inside that range. One WPQR commonly supports several WPS, each written to fall within what was qualified. Anything outside the range needs a new qualification.

A procedure test is the most common route, not the only one. ISO 15607 also allows qualification by tested consumables, previous experience, a standard welding procedure or a pre-production test — the WPS then rests on that evidence instead of a WPQR from ISO 15614.

ISO vs ASME

Same Idea, One Real Difference

ISO and ASME procedure qualification compared
Point ISO ASME
Qualification record WPQR PQR
Standards ISO 15607, 15609 and 15614 families BPVC Section IX
Where the ranges live In the WPQR, as its range of qualification. The WPS must sit inside it. The PQR records the actual test values. The ranges are stated on the WPS, under Section IX's variable rules.

Watch the letters "WPQR": in ISO it is the procedure record, but on ASME jobs the same letters are sometimes used for a welder's performance qualification. Check which one a document means before you file it.

In Practice

Where the Chain Breaks

  • A WPS revised without checking its WPQR. A new thickness or position slips outside the qualified range and nobody notices.
  • A WPS with no WPQR on file. The procedure exists; the evidence behind it is in someone's inbox.
  • A spreadsheet as the register. Every revision retyped by hand, with no link from a WPS to the record that qualifies it.

The fix is a register where each WPS points to its WPQR and every value can be searched and compared. Questions & Answers

pWPS, WPQR and WPS, Answered

Is a WPQR the same as a PQR?

Yes — WPQR is the ISO name, PQR the ASME name, for the record of a procedure qualification test. The practical difference is where the qualified ranges are written: in the WPQR under ISO, on the WPS under ASME.

Can one WPQR support several WPS?

Yes. A WPQR qualifies a range, so any number of WPS can rest on it as long as each stays inside that range. A shop usually has far more WPS than WPQRs.

Do I always need a pWPS?

Only when you qualify a new procedure by test. If an existing WPQR already covers the job, you write the WPS directly from it.

Where does the WPQ fit in?

The WPQ qualifies the welder, not the procedure. It has its own rules for staying valid — see welder qualification validity, continuity and revalidation.

Standards referenced

  • ISO 15609-1 — welding procedure specification, arc welding.
  • ISO 15614-1 — welding procedure test, arc and gas welding of steels and arc welding of nickel.
  • ASME BPVC Section IX — welding, brazing and fusing qualifications.
