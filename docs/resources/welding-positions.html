Welding Positions:
ISO 6947 and ASME, in One Chart.

PA to J-L045, 1G to 6G, 1F to 5F: every position for plate and pipe, butt and fillet, drawn side by side.

By the Delteris team · Last reviewed 24 October 2026 · All guides

The Short Answer

ISO 6947 names a welding position with letters: PA is flat, PB horizontal-vertical, PC horizontal, PD horizontal-overhead, PE overhead, PF vertical up and PG vertical down; for fixed pipe, PH welds upwards, PJ downwards, and H-L045 and J-L045 do the same on a pipe inclined at 45°. ASME Section IX and AWS use a number and a letter instead: 1 to 6 for the position, G for a groove (butt) weld and F for a fillet weld. The two systems mostly line up. The one real difference: ASME's 3G, 5G and 6G don't say up or down, which ISO puts in the code.

How to Read the Drawings

ISOASME

The big white code is ISO 6947; the yellow tag beside it is the ASME IX / AWS equivalent. So PF 3G ↑ reads “PF in ISO, 3G uphill in ASME”.

  • Yellow is the weld bead.
  • Blue is the direction of welding, or the pipe turning on rotated pipe.
  • Red is the torch or electrode.
  • No blue arrow: the welding direction doesn’t change the position.

Reading the Codes

What the Letters and Numbers Mean

  • ISO letters name the main positions. PA (flat) to PG (vertical down) for plate. PH and PJ are fixed pipe with its axis horizontal, welding up or down; PK is orbital welding of pipe, the position for mechanised orbital heads.
  • H-L045 and J-L045 are the inclined pipe. H welds upwards, J downwards, L045 is the 45° slope of the pipe axis. It's the most demanding fixed-pipe position, and a common choice for welder tests because it covers the most ground.
  • ASME numbers go 1 to 6. 1 flat, 2 horizontal, 3 vertical, 4 overhead for plate; for pipe, 1 is rotated, 2 has the axis vertical, 5 the axis horizontal and fixed, 6 the axis at 45° and fixed. G is a groove weld, F a fillet weld, and R in 2FR means the pipe is rotated.
  • Up or down lives in different places. ISO writes it in the code (PF up, PG down). ASME writes 3G for both and records the progression as its own variable, so a WPS or welder qualification to ASME says "uphill" or "downhill" separately.
  • Downhill has its own uses. Welding downwards (PG on plate, PJ and J-L045 on pipe) is common on pipelines with cellulosic electrodes and on thin sheet.
  • The test position decides what a welder may weld. One test usually covers several production positions, and the rules come from the qualification standard, not from ISO 6947: ISO 9606-1 and ASME Section IX (QW-461.9) each have a table of which test position covers which. The harder positions cover more, so a 6G or H-L045 pipe test covers most of them. Direction counts too: a test welded upwards doesn't cover welding downwards.
