The Register You Already Keep — Kept Current for You

WPS management software keeps your Welding Procedure Specifications organized, searchable and in one place — so you always know exactly which procedures you hold, and your master register is never out of date. Most shops already run their WPSs from one big spreadsheet register, because corporate mandates a spreadsheet. That register is the truth — and maintaining it by hand, WPS by WPS, is slow and error-prone. Elenchon WPS Manager treats your workbook as the kernel of truth: you upload it, the app visualises it, and as new or revised WPSs come in — as Excel or PDF — it proposes the exact cell edits, waits for your approval, and patches only the cells you confirm. Then it exports the updated register, plus a folder of the linked WPS files, whenever you need it. It is the WPS engine from the Elenchon platform, offered standalone in the cloud.

Import — Excel & PDF

Get Your WPSs In — In Minutes, Not Weeks

Upload the WPS documents you already have, in the format you already have them. The parser does the data entry for you.

Most WPS tools make you re-type every procedure into their format, or wrestle a fragile Excel template — turning a folder of existing WPSs into days or weeks of work. Elenchon WPS Manager reads your documents instead, whether they arrive as a spreadsheet or a digital PDF form.

  • Excel and PDF, both native: Two purpose-built parsers — one for Excel WPS workbooks, one that reconstructs a PDF form's ruled grid and reads its cells. Process, materials, positions, ranges, pass-by-pass parameters and more land as structured fields, automatically.
  • Reads bilingual forms out of the box: The built-in profile understands English and Portuguese WPS layouts as shipped, so a mixed EN/PT form imports without special setup.
  • Batch import: Bring in a whole stack of existing WPSs at once, not one form at a time.
  • Heat input filled where it's blank: If a form leaves the heat-input column empty, the app computes it from the arc parameters (current × voltage × 60 ÷ travel speed × process efficiency, per EN 1011-1) and flags it for you to confirm against the PQR — never a silent number.
  • Tuned to your template: Standard ISO 15609-style forms work immediately; during onboarding we tune the parser to your company's exact layout, so it reads your paperwork — not a generic form you'd have to conform to.
Human-Reviewed Edits

Nothing Touches Your Register Until You Approve It

Every import proposes a change-set. You see the current value, the new value, and decide — cell by cell.

A register is only trustworthy if you control what goes into it. So imports don't overwrite your sheet — they queue as proposed edits you review and confirm.

  • See exactly what would change: The review queue lays out each proposed edit as current value → new value, down to the individual cell. Tick a cell to apply it, untick to leave it as it was.
  • Correct before you commit: Fix any value inline, or add a row for a field the parser missed — then approve. What lands is what you signed off on.
  • Low-confidence reads are flagged: Any cell the parser wasn't sure of — or a heat input it calculated — is shaded amber and surfaced for a check, so your attention goes exactly where it's needed and confident values don't waste your time.
  • Set your own approval bar: Choose how imports land — review every value, review only the uncertain ones (the default), or auto-apply everything and lean on versioning to undo. Your register, your rules.
  • Drift detection: If a WPS is edited after its values already landed, the app tells you the register is out of sync with the library and offers to re-sync — so the sheet never quietly drifts from the documents behind it.
  • Only approved cells are patched: Your workbook is never rebuilt from scratch. The app writes the confirmed cells into your own file and leaves everything else exactly as you had it.

The WPS Library

Not a Folder of Files — a Library That Understands Them

Every WPS, pWPS and WPQR, parsed into structured records you can search, compare, correct and link.

This is where the register earns its keep. Behind every row is a fully-read document you can open, verify against its source, edit in place, and connect to the records that qualify it — the small conveniences that turn a spreadsheet into a system.

  • Three linked document types, one home: WPS, preliminary WPS (pWPS) and WPQR each get their own tab, each imported the same way — drop an Excel or PDF, or add a WPQR record by hand. Every document sits alongside the procedures it relates to.
  • Revisions, grouped automatically: Several revisions of the same WPS number collapse into a single row with an obvious “N revisions” chip and a switcher to move between them — newest first. On import, each incoming file is classified for you as a brand-new WPS, a fresh revision, or a correction to an existing one.
  • Edit and verify in place: Correct any field's value — or even its label — and save; the label re-titles that column in the register. A one-click “Verify” clears a low-confidence flag once you've checked it, and computed heat input gets its own verify so the amber clears across every pass at once.
  • The real detail, not just a filename: Header fields, the full pass table with each pass's side (inside / outside / both) and role (root / fill / cap / backing), base-material parts, filler and consumable, shielding and flux — all extracted, all editable, with a row you can add if the parser ever misses one.

And when you need to find something, the whole library becomes a database you can question — filtered by what's actually inside each document, not its filename.

  • Filter by what matters: Narrow by welding process, base or filler material, position, thickness range and more — down to a specific combination of processes, with or without a backing pass, in a couple of clicks.
  • Facets that read the pass table: Filter on the values inside the passes themselves — side, role, process, polarity — as set-membership filters, so “every WPS that runs a 121 root” is one click, not a manual read.
  • Document facets too: Slice by parse state, file type (Excel or PDF), template, link status, or whether a WPS has a linked PQR — the practical questions a QA lead actually asks.
  • Smart linking & the whole chain: Link a WPS to the WPQR that qualifies it, a WPQR to the several WPS it supports, a pWPS to the number it becomes — then see the related documents together and open any of them in a click. Give a WPQR a friendly display name so it reads cleanly everywhere.
  • Always know what you hold: One central library means you always know the full set of procedures your shop has qualified — nothing lost in someone's inbox or a shared drive.
Preview Mode

See Every Read Against the Original — Then Fix It Right There

The parsed fields on one side, your actual document on the other. Trust nothing on faith; confirm everything at a glance.

This is the feature that makes a parser safe to rely on. Open any WPS in Preview and the app splits the screen: on the left, every field it read; on the right, your real file — the PDF form rendered in place, or the Excel sheet drawn inline. Your eye runs down one column and across to the other, and in seconds you know the register matches the paperwork.

  • The genuine source, not a re-render: A PDF opens as the real PDF, page and all, with its own zoom and fit-to-width; an Excel WPS renders as the actual sheet. You're checking against the document your welding engineer signed, not the app's interpretation of it.
  • Built to live in, not glance at: Preview is a verification workspace — it stays open while you work through a document and won't vanish on a stray click outside it. Widen the source pane with a draggable divider when a form is dense, up to half the screen.
  • Correct without leaving: Spot a misread? Edit the value — or its label — right inside Preview, tick a low-confidence field as verified, accept a computed heat input, and save. No bouncing between the file and a separate edit screen; compare and fix are the same view.
  • Everything stays in sync: A fix in Preview updates the library detail, the register column titles, and the review badge together — one correction, reflected everywhere it should be.

It's a small thing that changes how the whole tool feels: you're never asked to trust a number you haven't seen on the page yourself.

The Whole Linked Set

Every Record Behind a WPS, Attached and Visible

See the WPQR that qualifies each WPS, and the pWPS behind that WPQR — the full document chain, linked in one place.

A WPS never stands alone: it's qualified by a WPQR, which in turn traces back to a preliminary WPS (pWPS). Elenchon WPS Manager keeps that whole chain attached, so from any WPS you can see exactly which records support it — no hunting through folders.

  • WPQR attached to the WPS: Open any WPS and see the Procedure Qualification Record that qualifies it, attached and displayed in a clean, readable layout.
  • pWPS attached to the WPQR: Trace each WPQR back to the preliminary WPS behind it — the full document chain, pWPS to WPQR to WPS, linked and visible.
  • Attach any supporting file: Keep the related documents together with the procedure they belong to, so the paperwork trail is always one click away, not scattered across drives.

Note: WPS Manager attaches, links and displays these records — capturing qualification data live on the shop floor, and deriving qualified ranges from it, is the job of the full Elenchon platform.

Heat Input Helper

Stay Inside the Window Your PQR Qualified

A live scratch pad for the one calculation welders get wrong under pressure.

Heat input is where a perfectly good procedure quietly goes out of qualification. The built-in helper makes the safe window impossible to miss.

  • Set the qualified range, then explore: Enter the heat-input range your PQR qualified for each weld region — root, fill, cap — then drag the current, voltage and travel-speed sliders to see the resulting heat input computed live.
  • Sliders that can't leave the window: The controls clamp to your PQR-qualified range, so you physically can't dial in a combination that falls outside what the procedure allows.
  • Multi-arc aware: Tandem and twin-arc setups get a lead and a trail head with their own current and voltage; the total heat input is summed across heads, the way the standard expects.
  • Pull straight from a WPS: Load a parsed WPS from your library and its per-region ranges and processes fill in automatically — the arc parameters stay yours to play with. It's a scratch pad; nothing here is saved or written to any sheet.

Questions & Answers

WPS Management Software, Answered

What Elenchon WPS Manager does, how import works, and where it fits.

What is WPS management software?

WPS management software keeps your Welding Procedure Specifications organized, searchable and in one place, so you always know which procedures your shop holds and can find any one instantly. Elenchon WPS Manager works from the master register most shops already keep: it imports your existing WPS documents, extracts every field, proposes reviewed edits to your register, and exports the updated workbook and its linked files whenever you need them.

Can it import Excel and PDF WPSs?

Yes — both, natively. One parser reads Excel WPS workbooks; another reconstructs a PDF form's grid and reads its cells. Either way, process, materials, ranges and pass-by-pass parameters land as structured fields, and the built-in profile reads English and Portuguese forms out of the box. Where a form leaves heat input blank, the app calculates it from the arc parameters and flags it for you to confirm.

Does an import change my register automatically?

Only if you choose to let it. By default every import queues as a proposed change-set: you see the current value beside the new value, cell by cell, and approve what lands — with low-confidence reads shaded for a closer look. You can set the bar higher (review every value) or lower (auto-apply and rely on versioning to undo). Only the cells you approve are patched into your own workbook; nothing is rebuilt.

Can I filter or search my WPSs by welding process?

Yes. Because every field is extracted at import, you filter your library by real criteria — welding process, base and filler material, position, thickness range and more. You can narrow it down to a single process, or a specific combination of processes with or without a backing pass, in a couple of clicks, instead of scrolling filenames.

Does WPS Manager create WPSs, or just manage them?

Today its job is management — import, review, organize, filter, version, and export, with supporting WPQRs and pWPSs kept alongside. It already exports a clean Excel workbook per WPS (or the whole register), and that file re-imports losslessly. A spreadsheet-style builder for writing and bulk-editing WPSs in the cloud is on the roadmap. It deliberately does not derive qualified ranges from test data — that metallurgy is handled by the full Elenchon qualification platform.

How much does Elenchon WPS Manager cost?

€80 per month, or €720 per year (an effective €60 per month) billed annually. One flat, fully published price — no per-seat charges and no "contact us for a quote." It includes 50 GB of storage (tens of thousands of WPSs); extra 50 GB blocks are €10/month if you ever need them. One tenancy is one system — we recommend one instance per isolated factory but don't police it. Onboarding and parser setup for your template are included and handled directly by the founders.

How is it different from the Elenchon platform?

Elenchon WPS Manager is the cloud WPS register on its own — for shops that just need their procedures organized, current and searchable. The full Elenchon platform adds shop-floor WPQR capture, range qualification, welder continuity (WPQ) and full traceability on an on-premise appliance. Same team; WPS Manager is the lightweight cloud slice, and it's the natural starting point if you later grow into the full platform.
