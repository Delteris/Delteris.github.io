Get Your WPSs In — In Minutes, Not Weeks

WPS import software reads your existing Welding Procedure Specifications — Excel workbooks or PDF forms — and extracts every field into structured, searchable records, so you never re-type a procedure to get it into your register. Most WPS tools make you conform to their template, or wrestle a fragile Excel form, turning a stack of existing WPSs into days of work. Elenchon WPS Manager works the other way round: it reads your documents. A digital PDF form is reconstructed grid-line by grid-line and read cell by cell; an Excel WPS is parsed straight from the workbook. The parser reads a form's structure rather than a fixed vocabulary, so it isn't tied to one language — it imports whole batches at once and is tuned to your company's exact form and wording during onboarding, so what lands is your paperwork, structured, not a generic template you had to fill in.

Two Native Parsers

Excel and PDF, Both First-Class

Not a converter bolted on — two purpose-built readers, one for each format your WPSs actually arrive in.

A WPS reaches you as a spreadsheet or as a signed PDF form. WPS Manager reads both natively, so neither has to be flattened, rekeyed, or re-drawn to get in.

  • PDF forms, reconstructed and read: The PDF parser rebuilds the form's ruled grid and reads each cell in place — header fields, the pass table, ranges — from the digital document your welding engineer produced.
  • Excel workbooks, parsed straight: The Excel parser reads a WPS workbook directly, mapping its cells to structured fields without a fragile intermediate template.
  • Every field, structured: Process, base and filler materials, positions, thickness and parameter ranges, and pass-by-pass values all become typed fields you can search, filter, compare and export — not a flat scan.
Language-Agnostic by Design

It Reads the Form's Structure, Not a Fixed Vocabulary

The parser matches your labels in whatever language they're written — so a form in your language, or a mixed-language one, reads cleanly.

Shops that work across borders rarely have a single-language form. WPS Manager doesn't hard-code one language: it reads the structure of an ISO 15609-style form and matches its labels from a language pack, so what the labels are written in doesn't change whether they're understood.

  • Structure first, words second: The parser recognises the layout of a WPS — header block, pass table, ranges — and reads it correctly regardless of the label language, so a form in your language maps on the first pass.
  • Your language, your wording: Labels are matched from a per-customer language pack, so your in-house terms and your language are added by supplying the terms — no rebuilt template, no code change.
  • Shipped and extensible: English and Portuguese ship today; any further language or shop-specific vocabulary is added the same way during onboarding.
Batch Import

A Whole Stack at Once, Not One Form at a Time

Bring years of accumulated WPSs into the register in a single pass.

The first job is usually the biggest: getting everything you already hold into one place. Batch import is built for exactly that — drop the folder, not the file.

  • Import in bulk: Bring in a whole stack of existing WPSs at once instead of adding them one by one, so the initial load is measured in a sitting, not a season.
  • Classified as they land: Each incoming file is sorted for you into a brand-new WPS, a fresh revision of one you already hold, or a correction to an existing record — so revisions group correctly from the start.
  • Every field still reviewable: Bulk speed doesn't skip the check — each imported document still surfaces its fields for review, with anything uncertain flagged (see reviewed edits).
Nothing Left Blank Silently

Heat Input Computed Where the Form Omits It

A blank heat-input column is filled from the arc parameters — and flagged, never slipped in quietly.

Many WPS forms leave heat input off the sheet, expecting it to be derived. Rather than import a hole, WPS Manager computes it — and tells you it did, so you can check it against the PQR.

  • Calculated from the arc parameters: Where the heat-input cell is empty, the app computes it as current × voltage × 60 ÷ travel speed × process efficiency, per EN 1011-1.
  • Always flagged, never silent: A computed heat input is shaded and surfaced for confirmation against the PQR — a number you can trust because you can see where it came from.
  • Cleared in one move: Once you've confirmed it, a single verify clears the flag across every pass at once (see preview & verification).

Questions & Answers

WPS Import, Answered

What the parser reads, which formats it takes, and how it fits your forms.

Is this OCR, or does it actually read the fields?

It reads the fields. A digital PDF is reconstructed grid-line by grid-line and read cell by cell; an Excel WPS is parsed straight from the workbook. Process, materials, positions, ranges and every pass parameter land as typed, searchable values you can filter and export — not a flat scan you still have to read by eye.

What if my WPSs aren't in English?

The parser reads a form's structure, not a fixed set of English words, and matches your labels from a language pack — so the language on the form doesn't change whether it's understood. English and Portuguese ship today; your language and in-house wording are added during onboarding by supplying the terms, with no code change.

How accurate is the extraction — and what if it misreads?

Anything the parser isn't sure of is flagged, not guessed silently, and every import is reviewed before it lands. You confirm reads against the original document in Preview and correct a misread in place, so what ends up on record is what a person checked — not the parser's raw output.

What happens when a form leaves heat input blank?

The app computes it from the arc parameters (current × voltage × 60 ÷ travel speed × process efficiency, per EN 1011-1) and flags it for you to confirm against the PQR. It's never a silent number — you see the passes it came from and clear the flag once checked.

I have years of accumulated WPSs. Can I bring them all in at once?

Yes — that first bulk load is exactly what batch import is for. Drop the whole stack in one pass; each file is sorted as it lands into a new WPS, a new revision of one you hold, or a correction, so revisions group correctly from the start instead of sprawling into duplicates.

Will it read our specific form, or just a generic one?

Standard ISO 15609-style forms read out of the box; your exact layout is tuned in before you go live. You send a few real WPSs, we confirm the parser reads them and adjust the profile if needed, and only then do you commit — so import is proven on your own paperwork, handled in strict confidence.
