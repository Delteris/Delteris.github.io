Buyer's Guide

How to Choose
Welding Management Software.

Most welding software is capable on features. What decides how it serves you for years is what a checklist hides: whether it takes work off your hands, how it handles the qualification records your code demands, where your records live and what it really costs.

Look Past the Feature Checklist

Choose welding management software on the questions a feature checklist won't answer: does it remove work or just move paperwork onto a screen, does it keep the qualification chain linked, where do your records physically live, and can you model the full cost before you sign? Almost every tool can store a WPS, a welder qualification and a weld record. What separates them is everything underneath.

What to Look For

The Questions That Decide It

Ask every vendor on your shortlist the same questions. They matter long after the demo.

Score every tool on the same list, not on feature count.

  • Does it do the work, or digitise the paperwork? Good tools derive a welder's continuity from real production entries, show a worker the exact joint to weld, and build the audit trail as work happens. Others put the same filing on a screen. Ask where it removes effort.
  • Does it keep the qualification chain linked? Each WPS should point to the WPQR (or ASME PQR) behind it and to the qualified welders, with the continuity clock running. Storing the documents isn't enough. Background: pWPS vs WPQR vs WPS and welder qualification validity.
  • Does it reach inspection and PWHT? NDT (VT, RT, UT, MT, PT) and post-weld heat treatment carry records an auditor expects tied to the joint — heavily under ASME pressure work, less under light EN 1090 structural work. Ask exactly how far coverage goes.
  • Where do your records physically live? Your hardware or a vendor's cloud decides data sovereignty, outage behaviour and third-party-processor questions — increasingly relevant under NIS2. Most tools are cloud-only; ask if on-premise exists, and who maintains it.
  • Is the price published? Get the flat number, per-seat behaviour, setup fees and every add-on in writing before a sales call. If total cost can't be modelled up front, that is the answer.
  • Does capture survive a dropped connection? Shop floors have dead zones. Ask whether capture is offline-first or stops with the link.
  • What happens if you leave? Do you keep view-only access to your history for audits, or does it end with the contract?
  • Does it cover the floor, or one slice? Procedures, qualifications, material traceability, equipment calibration (ISO 17662) and non-weld work in one structure — or several tools stitched by hand?
Vendor Context

Three Kinds of Tool, Compared

Welding software clusters into a few archetypes. Knowing which one you are looking at tells you most of what you need before the demo.

Rather than a brand-by-brand table, it helps to place a tool into one of three broad categories — because each carries predictable strengths and trade-offs that a feature list won't surface.

No archetype is universally right — a two-person WPS writer is overkill for one shop and nowhere near enough for another. Match the category to how your floor actually works and which codes you are held to, then compare specific products within it.

Questions & Answers

Choosing Welding Software, Answered

Common questions when comparing tools.

What should I look for in welding management software?

Weigh what is hard to change later: whether the tool reduces work or just digitises paperwork, how it maintains the qualification chain (WPS to WPQR/PQR to welder qualification) and the continuity clock the codes require, how far it reaches into NDT and PWHT, where your records live, and whether the price is published.

How do I know a tool will actually hold up in an audit?

Ask to see it produce evidence, not store it. It passes when any weld traces in one pull to its material heat number, the WPS and qualification record behind it (WPS to WPQR/PQR, per ISO 15614 / ASME IX), the welder, and the inspection result, with a clear change history. If that means exporting from several places and stitching it by hand, it is a filing cabinet.

How is welder qualification and continuity tracked?

A welder's qualification (ISO 9606, or performance qualification under ASME IX) certifies a range of work, and most codes require continuity — evidence of welding within that range, commonly on a six-month interval under ISO 9606. Capable software derives continuity from real production entries and warns before a qualification lapses.

Should welding software handle NDT and PWHT?

It depends on your work. Non-destructive testing (visual, RT, UT, MT, PT) and post-weld heat treatment carry records an auditor expects tied to the joint — heavily for pressure work under ASME, less for light structural work under EN 1090. Decide which downstream records your code demands, then ask each vendor exactly how far their coverage goes.

Is on-premise or cloud better for welding records?

Neither is universally better. Cloud needs no hardware, but records sit on shared infrastructure and the bill often scales per seat. On-premise keeps records on hardware you own, with predictable cost and stronger data control, at the price of owning an appliance.
