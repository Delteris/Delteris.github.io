Traceability Is the Whole Point

Welding traceability software records the full chain of custody behind every weld — the base material and its heat number, the welder, the qualified procedure, and the result — so any joint can be traced from finished piece back to raw material, and forward again. In fabrication, the unit an auditor cares about is the joint, and traceability accumulates upward — from base-material leaves carrying their heat numbers, into the assemblies those parts form, up to the finished piece. Every question worth asking reduces to one: prove this weld is sound, and prove who touched it. Elenchon is built so that proof is a by-product of everyday work, not a paperwork exercise bolted on afterward. It is the sovereign, excellence-first standard for weld traceability — the full chain of custody on hardware you own, never a shared cloud.

The Traceable Chain

From Material In to Finished Piece

The links that connect a heat number to a completed weld — captured once, followed forever.

Traceability is only real if every link in the chain is captured and connected. Elenchon builds the chain as work happens, so it is complete by the time anyone needs it.

  • Heat numbers at the root: Base material is captured with its heat number, linked to its parent MTR, and propagated down the project tree — full material traceability without database bloat.
  • Attributed at every step: Each capture is tied to the person who made it and the work type it belongs to, so the chain records not just what happened but who did it.
  • Qualification-linked: Welds connect to the WPS they follow and the welder's WPQ, so a joint carries proof it was made to a qualified procedure by a qualified hand.
  • Both directions: Follow the chain down from a finished piece to its materials, or up from a heat number to every weld it ended up in.

Trustworthy by Construction

A Record That Can't Quietly Change

Traceability is only worth anything if the trail can't be edited after the fact.

A trustworthy audit trail keeps its history. Elenchon versions and verifies the record as work happens, so the chain stands up to scrutiny.

  • Version-pinned on capture: Every document, parameter and consumable is versioned the moment it is captured, so the state at any point in time is recoverable.
  • Versioned corrections: Captured records are versioned, and any correction is versioned too and stays visible to managers — a clean history with no administrative bottleneck.
  • Captured nodes protected: Any node that already holds captured work is automatically locked, so live project edits can never overwrite history.

Questions & Answers

Welding Traceability, Answered

What traceability means, and how Elenchon builds it on your own hardware.

How does heat-number traceability work?

Base material is captured with its heat number and linked to its parent MTR, then propagated down the project tree so every part and assembly built from it inherits the link — giving you material traceability from goods-in to the finished weld without duplicating data everywhere.

Can the traceability record be edited after the fact?

The history is preserved. A captured record is versioned as it lands; if something needs correcting, the correction is versioned too and stays visible to managers, and any node holding captured work is protected from accidental overwrites. So the chain keeps its history — which is what makes it audit-grade.

Does it help with ISO 3834 and ASME IX audits?

Yes. Because traceability is captured and version-pinned as work happens, it doubles as audit evidence for codes such as ISO 3834, EN 1090 and ASME IX — following any weld to its materials, welder and procedure is instant.

How is weld traceability different from weld tracking?

They are usually the same idea: "weld tracking" tends to emphasise following a weld's status through production, while "traceability" emphasises the full evidence chain behind it. Elenchon does both — it tracks work as it happens and preserves the traceable chain that proves it.

What is welding traceability software?

It is software that records the full chain of custody behind every weld — the base material and its heat number, the person who welded it, the procedure they followed, and the result — so any joint can be traced from finished piece back to raw material, and forward again. Elenchon runs that traceability on-premise, on hardware you own.

Is an on-premise chain of custody a compromise versus cloud?

No — for a chain of custody it's the stronger position. Your evidence never touches a shared cloud server, so there's no third-party processor between you and the record an auditor asks to see, and no cross-border question over where it lives. Reach isn't sacrificed: authorised staff and auditors connect from anywhere through Phrakton's zero-trust, post-quantum-safe overlay.
