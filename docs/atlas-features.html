Import

Excel & PDF, Read For You

Two purpose-built parsers do the data entry — no re-typing a procedure into someone else's template.

01
  • Excel and PDF, both native: One parser reads Excel WPS workbooks; another reconstructs an exported PDF form's ruled grid and reads its cells (scans aren't read). Process, materials, ranges and pass-by-pass parameters land as structured fields.
  • Language-agnostic: The parser reads a form's structure, not a fixed vocabulary, and matches labels by their wording — so a form in your language reads without special setup. English, Portuguese and Spanish labels are built in; any other wording is placed once and remembered for your organisation.
  • Batch, and classified as it lands: Bring in a whole stack at once — each file sorted into a new WPS, a new revision, or a correction, with revisions grouped from the start.
  • Adapts to your forms: No template to pick. The parser works out each form's layout from the file, ISO 15609-1, ASME IX and AWS D1.1 forms included, and splits files that hold several WPSs.

Human-Reviewed Edits

Nothing Lands Until You Approve It

A parser is only trustworthy if you can see what it read. Every import queues as a change-set you sign off, value by value.

02
  • See exactly what would change: Each row is a document, a field, and the value read — nothing is written to your register until you confirm it.
  • Low-confidence reads are flagged: Anything the parser wasn't sure of — or a heat input it computed — is shaded amber and surfaced, so your attention goes where it's needed.
  • Verify without leaving: Confirm a computed value from the queue, or open the document in the library to check it against the source first.
  • Set your own bar: Review every value, review only the uncertain ones (the default), or auto-apply and rely on versioning to undo.

A Library That Reads Your WPS

Every Field, Down to the Pass Table

Behind every register row is a fully-read document — header fields, the full pass table, materials and consumables, all structured and all searchable.

04
  • Three document types, one home: WPS, pWPS and WPQR each get a tab — WPS and pWPS parsed from their files, each WPQR added with its certificate — all linkable, with Parsed and Linked status at a glance.
  • The real detail: Header block — standard, base metal, thickness and diameter range, preheat, shielding — plus the full pass table with process, filler class, polarity, current, voltage, travel speed and heat input for every pass.
  • Edit and verify in place: Correct any value or its label and save; a one-click verify clears a low-confidence flag, and computed heat input clears across every pass at once.
  • Filter by what's inside: Narrow by process, material, position and thickness — even by values inside the passes, such as every WPS that runs a 111 root.

The Linked Chain

WPQR and pWPS, Attached to the Procedure

A WPS is qualified by a WPQR, which traces back to a pWPS. The library keeps that chain linked, so from any record you can see exactly what supports it.

05
  • WPQR linked to the WPS it qualifies: A single WPQR can support several procedures, each linked to the WPS numbers it qualifies, picked from the library by number.
  • pWPS linked to the WPQR: Trace each qualification record back to the preliminary WPS behind it — the full pWPS → WPQR → WPS chain, visible and one click apart.
  • Collected data, structured: Qualification standard, process, test date and the rest are read into fields, not left buried in an attachment.
  • Where Atlas stops: It attaches, links and displays these records — capturing WPQR results live on the floor is the job of the full Elenchon platform.

WPQR Range Check

Every WPS Checked Against Its WPQR

Enter the range of qualification once from each WPQR certificate. Atlas then checks every WPS that cites it, field by field, and shows you what sits outside.

06
  • Field by field: processes, material groups, thickness, diameter, positions, preheat, interpass, PWHT, current, consumables and heat input — each marked inside, outside, differs or not checked.
  • One WPQR per run group, by default: each run group (process and filler) has to be covered by one WPQR on every field. Combining WPQRs into one wider range is an opt-in per WPS, and every combined result is labelled.
  • No guessing: a WPS with no preheat minimum is never assumed to be at room temperature, and a blank or unclear value is “not checked”, not a red flag.
  • Where Atlas stops: it compares the values you entered from your certificates. It doesn't derive ranges from ISO 15614-1 or decide whether a WPS is qualified — a different consumable shows as “differs”, for the person responsible to judge.

Welder Coverage

Who Can Weld This WPS

Atlas matches each WPS to the welders whose current certificates cover it, and each welder to the WPS they can weld.

07
  • On every WPS: the welders qualified for it, the certificate that covers each one, and who holds the process but isn't covered.
  • On every welder: the WPS their current qualifications cover — open any welder's record to see it.
  • Nobody qualified? You see it on the WPS, and the monthly report lists every WPS with no qualified welder.
  • Compared, not judged: results come from the range typed from each certificate; Atlas doesn't derive ranges from ISO 9606-1 or ASME IX.

Heat Input Helper

Stay Inside the Qualified Window

Heat input is where a good procedure quietly goes out of qualification. The helper computes it live and clamps the controls to the range your WPQR qualified.

08
  • Pull straight from a WPS: Load a parsed WPS and its per-region ranges and processes fill in automatically — here, the arc values from MER-WPS-026.
  • Sliders that can't leave the window: Drag current, voltage and travel speed and watch the heat input compute live; the controls clamp to your WPQR-qualified range, so an out-of-range result can't be dialled in.
  • Root, fill and cap independently: Each weld region gets its own qualified range and its own live read — green when it's inside the range.
  • Multi-arc aware: Tandem and twin-arc setups sum across heads, the way the standard expects. It's a scratch pad — nothing is saved or written to any sheet.

Two Clocks & Warnings

A Lapse You See Coming

Expiry and continuity tracked as two separate dates, warned ahead on the dashboard and — if you switch it on — by email.

10
  • Two clocks, watched apart: an expiry date and a continuity date, each with its own warning tiers — a month out, two weeks, the week of — at lead times you set.
  • The dashboard flags what’s due: a count of qualifications entering a warning period, one click to filter the grid to exactly who.
  • Email reminders, opt-in: add up to ten recipients — the QM, a supervisor, whoever plans the floor — to get the heads-up in their inbox ahead of each deadline.
  • It records and warns: Atlas surfaces the dates and the facts; whether a qualification is valid for a job stays a human call.

Monthly Report

Every Month, What Changed and What Needs Attention

On by default. Each month Atlas freezes a snapshot of your register and your welders, so you can see what moved — and generate one on demand whenever you need it.

12
  • Range check across the register: how many WPS were checked, and how many sit outside, differ or weren't checked.
  • What changed: new problems and resolved ones since the last report.
  • Welder qualifications: lapsed, expiring within a month, confirmations due and expired this month.
  • Welder coverage: the WPS with no qualified welder, and every WPS needing attention, one click away. Export the whole report as a PDF.

What You See

  • Saved to your account: the setting follows you to any browser.
  • Nothing forgotten: after each sign-in, a short note lists what you've hidden, with a link to switch it back on.
