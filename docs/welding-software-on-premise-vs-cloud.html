What Actually Separates Them

The practical difference between on-premise and cloud welding software is not the feature list — it is where your records physically live, how access is priced over the years, and whether the system keeps working when the internet doesn't. Both can run a good traceability operation, and we won't pretend a capable cloud tool isn't capable. But those three questions — ownership, cost, and how much of your operation depends on someone else's uptime — don't show up in a feature checklist, and they're the ones that decide how the system serves you for years after you sign. On every one of them, on-premise is the stronger answer.

The Structural Difference

Why Shops Choose On-Premise

Advantages that come from where the software runs — not a feature list a cloud tool can simply match.

Cloud welding tools are capable, and if you have no wish to own hardware, one may suit you — we won't pretend otherwise. But for a shop that takes ownership, cost control and continuity seriously, these follow from the architecture itself, and they hold up over the life of the system.

  • You own the data, physically: Your records live on hardware in your building, never on a shared server. Cancel anytime and keep permanent view-only access for audits — no export scramble, no hostage data. A cloud subscription that ends can take your access with it.
  • A bill with no surprises: A flat rate per appliance with a generous block of workers included, extra seats at a published €9/month, and hardware at cost — every figure up front. No per-seat meter, no usage surcharge, no quote that only appears after a sales call.
  • Offline-first, reachable anywhere: Capture is built offline-first, so the floor keeps working with no connection at all and syncs on reconnect — a public-URL tool simply stops when its link does. On-site you're served over your own network; off-site, the optional Remote Access Module reaches you from home, mobile data or abroad through Phrakton's encrypted fabric.
  • Sovereign by design: With no data leaving the building and no open inbound ports, you sidestep the third-party-processor and cross-border questions a cloud service inevitably raises — and stay invisible to the public internet.
  • The full platform, no lock-in: Every capability runs on-premise — not a stripped-down local edition — with no minimum term and no exit fee. What you build stays yours.
Honest Guidance

Which Should You Choose?

If you care about owning your data outright, a cost your finance team can fix in advance, and a system that keeps running whether or not the internet does, an on-premise appliance is the better fit — and Elenchon delivers the full platform, not a stripped-down local edition. Cloud has its place: if you have no wish to own any hardware and are comfortable with your records on a vendor's infrastructure and a bill that scales with your team, it will serve you. But for most fabrication shops that take audits, cost and continuity seriously, the ownership and predictability of on-premise is decisive — which is exactly what we built.

Questions & Answers

On-Premise vs. Cloud, Answered

The practical questions shops ask when weighing the two.

Is on-premise welding software harder to run than cloud?

Less than shops expect. Elenchon ships as a containerized appliance that our own engineers deliver, install and configure on-site, so there's no IT project to staff. Day to day, on-site users just open the app on your network, and — with the optional Remote Access Module — anyone off-site, a manager at home or an auditor on mobile data, reaches it just as easily through Phrakton, with nothing to configure and no ports to open.

Is cloud welding software less secure?

Not inherently — reputable cloud vendors invest heavily in security. The difference is exposure: a cloud service is reachable on the public internet and holds your data on shared infrastructure, while an on-premise appliance keeps data in your building and, with Elenchon, opens no inbound ports at all.

How does cost compare over time?

Cloud pricing is often per-seat or usage-based behind a quote, so the bill is hard to predict and grows as you add people or data. Elenchon publishes everything: a flat rate per appliance with 20 or 50 workers included, extra seats at €9/month, and hardware at cost. Both charge for scale — the difference is that ours is knowable in advance, down to the euro.

Can on-premise software still be reached remotely?

Yes. Elenchon's optional Phrakton overlay lets authorised staff and auditors connect through mutually authenticated, post-quantum-safe tunnels, without the appliance exposing any public address — remote access without cloud exposure.

What happens to my data if I stop paying?

With Elenchon, the data is on your hardware and you keep permanent view-only access to your history for audits. With many cloud tools, access ends when the subscription does — worth confirming with any vendor you consider.

Does on-premise mean fewer features?

Not with Elenchon. It runs the full platform on-premise — traceability, the WPS/PQR/WPQ workflow, floor capture, equipment control and more — not a reduced local edition.
