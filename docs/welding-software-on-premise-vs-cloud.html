Fair Is the Point

Most fabrication shops evaluating welding software are choosing between cloud platforms and an on-premise system, and the honest answer is that both can run a good traceability operation. Cloud tools are well-built and feature-rich; we won't pretend otherwise. What differs is the architecture underneath — where your records physically live, how access is priced, and how much of your operation depends on someone else's uptime. Those differences don't show up in a feature checklist, but they are the ones that matter for years after you sign.

Credit Where It's Due

Where Cloud Tools Are Genuinely Strong

A fair comparison starts by naming what the other approach does well.

Cloud welding software earned its place. If these are your priorities, a cloud platform is a reasonable choice — and we'd rather you know that up front.

  • Nothing to host: No appliance on site, no local install — you sign in and go, which suits shops with no interest in owning any hardware.
  • Access from anywhere by default: A public URL is reachable from any network without setup, which some distributed teams value.
  • Mature, broad feature sets: Established cloud vendors have had years to build deep libraries and tooling, and it shows.
The Structural Difference

Where On-Premise Wins

The advantages that come from architecture, not feature count — and that a cloud tool can't simply add.

These aren't features you toggle on; they follow from where the software runs. That's why they hold up over the life of the system.

  • You own the data, physically: Records live on hardware in your building, not on a shared server. Cancel anytime and keep permanent view-only access for audits — no export scramble, no hostage data.
  • A bill that doesn't creep: One published flat rate per appliance, with hardware passed through at cost — not a per-seat subscription that grows every time you add a welder or renew a year.
  • Works when the internet doesn't: Production capture keeps running on your local network through an outage, with offline entries syncing on reconnect. A tool that lives on a public URL stops when the link does.
  • No lock-in: No minimum term, no exit fee, and your history stays with you if you leave.
  • Sovereign by design: With no data leaving the building and no open inbound ports, you sidestep the third-party-processor and cross-border questions a cloud service inevitably raises.
At a Glance

The Trade-Offs, Side by Side

Neither column is "wrong" — but the right-hand one is what shops choose Elenchon for.

  • Data location: Cloud — on the vendor's servers. On-premise — on hardware you own, in your facility.
  • Pricing shape: Cloud — often per-seat or usage-based, scaling with growth. On-premise (Elenchon) — flat, published, per appliance.
  • Offline operation: Cloud — dependent on the connection. On-premise — runs on your LAN, capture continues offline.
  • If you cancel: Cloud — access typically ends. On-premise (Elenchon) — permanent view-only access to your history.
  • Remote access: Cloud — built in via public URL. On-premise (Elenchon) — via Phrakton's zero-trust, post-quantum-safe overlay, with no open inbound ports.
Honest Guidance

Which Should You Choose?

If you have no wish to host anything and are comfortable with your records living on a vendor's infrastructure and a bill that scales with your team, a cloud platform will serve you. If you care about owning your data outright, a cost your finance team can fix in advance, and a floor that keeps capturing through an outage — an on-premise appliance is the better fit. Elenchon is built for the second shop, and it delivers the full platform, not a stripped-down local edition. For most fabrication shops that take audits and continuity seriously, that ownership and predictability is decisive.

Questions & Answers

On-Premise vs. Cloud, Answered

The practical questions shops ask when weighing the two.

Is on-premise welding software harder to run than cloud?

Less than shops expect. Elenchon ships as a containerized appliance that our own engineers deliver, install and configure on-site, so there's no IT project to staff. Day to day, users just open the app on the local network.

Is cloud welding software less secure?

Not inherently — reputable cloud vendors invest heavily in security. The difference is exposure: a cloud service is reachable on the public internet and holds your data on shared infrastructure, while an on-premise appliance keeps data in your building and, with Elenchon, opens no inbound ports at all.

How does cost compare over time?

Cloud pricing is often per-seat or usage-based, so the bill grows as you add people or data. Elenchon is a flat, published rate per appliance with hardware at cost, which makes total cost of ownership predictable years out. Which is cheaper depends on your size and horizon — but only one of them is knowable in advance.

Can on-premise software still be reached remotely?

Yes. Elenchon's optional Phrakton overlay lets authorised staff and auditors connect through mutually authenticated, post-quantum-safe tunnels, without the appliance exposing any public address — remote access without cloud exposure.

What happens to my data if I stop paying?

With Elenchon, the data is on your hardware and you keep permanent view-only access to your history for audits. With many cloud tools, access ends when the subscription does — worth confirming with any vendor you consider.

Does on-premise mean fewer features?

Not with Elenchon. It runs the full platform on-premise — traceability, the WPS/PQR/WPQ workflow, floor capture, equipment control and more — not a reduced local edition.
