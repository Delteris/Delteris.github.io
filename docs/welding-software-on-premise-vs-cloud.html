Decision Guide

On-Premise vs. Cloud
Welding Software.

Both can run a good welding quality operation. The choice turns on what a feature list won't show: where the records live, how the bill behaves over the years, what happens when the connection drops, and how easily you can leave. Here is how to weigh them.

By the Delteris team · We build one of each, so our bias is stated below · All guides

The Short Answer

Choose cloud when you want to start today with nothing to install and the job is keeping procedures and qualifications organised and findable. Choose on-premise when the records are audit-critical, traceability has to run down to the joint, work must carry on without a connection, or the data can't leave the building. For most shops the real objection to on-premise is the IT effort — and that is a question of how it is run, not where.

Factor by Factor

What Tips the Decision

Read across each row and note which column sounds like your shop.

Factors that favour cloud or on-premise welding software
Factor Favours cloud when… Favours on-premise when…
Scope The job is organising WPS, WPQR and welder qualifications. Traceability must run from the material to every joint, captured on the floor.
Ownership Records on the vendor's infrastructure are acceptable to you and your customers. Contracts, customers or regulators want the data in your building, with no third-party processor.
Cost over the years The team is small and stable, so the bill stays modest. Headcount grows or varies, and you want a flat cost you can model before you sign.
Connectivity Every place work is recorded has a reliable connection. There are Wi-Fi dead zones, or recording must continue when the line is down.
Leaving The vendor commits in writing to exports and access after you cancel. You want your history on your own hardware whatever happens to the subscription.
Effort to start You want to start today, with nothing to install. You can wait for an installation in exchange for owning the system.
Before You Sign

Five Questions for a Cloud Vendor

  • What will it cost at the headcount you expect in three years? Per-seat and usage pricing is easy to start and hard to predict.
  • Where is the data hosted, and under which jurisdiction? Your customers may ask.
  • What works when the connection drops? Ask about recording, not just viewing.
  • What happens to your access if you cancel? Get the answer in writing.
  • In what format can you export everything? Including attachments and history.

Questions & Answers

On-Premise vs. Cloud, Answered

Is on-premise or cloud cheaper?

It depends on time and headcount. Cloud usually costs less to start and more as people and data grow, especially when priced per seat. On-premise adds hardware and an installation up front, then a flat cost. Compare the total over three to five years at your expected team size, not the first invoice.

Is cloud welding software less secure?

Not automatically. A well-run cloud service can be very secure, but it is reachable on the public internet by design. An on-premise system can stay off the public internet entirely — if remote access is built without open ports. Ask either kind of vendor how access is secured.

Can on-premise software be reached from outside the shop?

It can, if the vendor provides a secure way in, such as an encrypted overlay network. Be wary of solutions that rely on open ports or a VPN you have to run yourself.

Do I need IT staff to run on-premise software?

Not if the vendor manages it: they install, update and repair the appliance remotely, and your part is keeping it powered. With classic self-hosted software, you do.

Can I start in the cloud and move on-premise later?

Ask the vendor how records move between the two before you commit. With our products, Atlas customers can move their WPS library and welder qualifications into Elenchon at launch.
