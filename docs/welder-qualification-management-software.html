What It Does

From a Drawer of Certificates to a Roster That Warns You

Track, warn, requalify and keep the history — the whole loop, in the cloud.

01

A Grid That Shows Status at a Glance

Welders in rows, processes in columns; each cell shows that process's soonest-due qualification, green to red. Filter by welder, process, standard, expiry or any field you've recorded, and open a welder for their full history.

02

Two Clocks, Watched Separately

Expiry and continuity are different deadlines, so Atlas tracks them as two dates and warns on each — a month out, two weeks, the week of. Lead times you set.

03

Warned Before It Lapses

The dashboard counts what's expiring soon; filter the grid to see who. Email reminders go to up to ten recipients — QM, supervisor, whoever plans the floor.

04

Requalification, Signed and Dated

Renew continuity behind a password signature — an immutable stamp: verified on this date, by this person, with your notes. Attribution on the record, not a validity ruling.

05

The Certificate, Side by Side

Attach the certificate — scan, PDF or image — to the record. Open any two files side by side, such as a welder's certificate next to the WPS it covers.

06

Nothing Overwritten

Every qualification a welder has ever held stays on file. Requalify or supersede, and the past record is kept — a full history per welder.

Variables come pre-built for ISO 9606, ASME IX and AWS. Working to another code, or need a field of your own? The record adapts — Atlas holds the data, it doesn't impose a standard.

Questions & Answers

Welder Qualification Management, Answered

What Atlas tracks, how it warns you, and where it fits.

What is welder qualification management software?

It tracks which welders hold which qualifications and when each expires or falls out of continuity, so you can plan requalifications ahead. Atlas stores a record per welder per qualification, warns you before each deadline on the dashboard and by email, and keeps the full history — instead of a drawer of certificates and a date in someone's head.

What are the two clocks — expiry and continuity?

A welder's ticket can end two ways: it reaches its expiry date, or the welder goes too long without welding to that qualification and loses continuity. They're different deadlines, so Atlas tracks them as two separate dates and warns on each independently. Full guide: welder qualification validity, continuity and revalidation.

How do the reminders work?

Every due date shows on your dashboard, colour-coded by how close it is. Switch on email reminders and add up to ten recipients — the QM, a supervisor, whoever plans the floor — and they're emailed ahead of each deadline, at lead times you set: a month out, two weeks, the week of.

Which standards does it support?

Qualification variables come pre-built for ISO 9606, ASME IX and AWS, so the fields you expect are already there. You can add your own fields, or track to another code — Atlas is oriented to the common standards but flexible enough to hold almost any qualification format.

Does it decide whether a welder is qualified?

No — and that's deliberate. Atlas records qualifications, tracks their dates, and warns you before they lapse. Whether a qualification is valid for a given job is a human call; the app gives you the facts and an attributed requalification record, it doesn't rule on validity.

How does it relate to WPS management?

Welder qualification is one half of Elenchon Atlas; WPS management is the other. Same cloud product, same flat price — your procedures and your qualified welders in one place. Shop-floor WPQR capture and continuity derived from logged work stay in the full Elenchon platform.
